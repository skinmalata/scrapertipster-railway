The Controversy

Not everyone is comfortable with the trend. Critics argue that the mass recruitment of diaspora players undermines the traditional link between a national team and its domestic football culture. When a team like Cape Verde fields 26 players who all play abroad â€” 14 of them born in Portugal â€” what does "Cape Verdean football" actually mean?

"There is a risk that national teams become disconnected from the countries they represent," said Dr. Christina Anderson, a sports sociologist at the University of Leicester. "Fans want to see players who have grown up in their culture, who understand the local context, who are part of the community. When the entire team is assembled from abroad, something is lost."

But the players themselves see it differently. "I grew up in France, but my parents are Algerian. Algeria is my country. Playing for Algeria is not a consolation prize. It is an honour," said Haller. For him, and for the 40-plus dual-nationality players at the 2026 World Cup, the decision is not about convenience. It is about identity. And as FIFA's rules continue to evolve, that identity will increasingly define the future of international football.

Nicolas Jackson's Double Golden Boot Chase: Can He Win Both the Premier League and AFCON Scoring Titles?

Nicolas Jackson is having the kind of season footballers dream about. The Senegalese striker, on loan at Bayern Munich from Chelsea, has been tearing through the Bundesliga with a goal tally that has the Premier League's elite looking nervously over their shoulders. And when the Africa Cup of Nations kicked off in December 2025, Jackson carried that red-hot form straight into the tournament, scoring a brace in Senegal's opening game. The result? People are starting to ask a question that would have seemed absurd two years ago: is Nicolas Jackson about to win both the Premier League Golden Boot and the AFCON Golden Boot in the same season? The premise itself is remarkable. No male footballer has ever won the Premier League Golden Boot and the AFCON Golden Boot simultaneously in a single season. Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang came close when he shared the Premier League Golden Boot in 2018-19 and won the AFCON Golden Boot in 2017 — but those were different seasons. Mohamed Salah won three Premier League Golden Boots between 2018 and 2022 and played in AFCON tournaments every two years, but he has never won the AFCON Golden Boot. Jackson is chasing something genuinely unprecedented. And the timing could not be more perfect.

"He has everything you need to be a top striker. Pace, power, finishing. But the thing that has improved most is his mentality. He believes he belongs with the best now." — Vincent Kompany, Bayern Munich head coach, March 2026

The Journey: From Ziguinchor to Munich

Jackson was born in Ziguinchor, a city in the Casamance region of southern Senegal, close to the border with Guinea-Bissau. He grew up playing football on the streets, idolising Thierry Henry and Didier Drogba. At 16, he moved to Spain to pursue his dream, joining the youth academy of Villarreal. It was a culture shock — a Senegalese teenager alone in a new country, learning a new language, adapting to a new style of football — but Jackson's talent was undeniable. He progressed through the ranks, made his senior debut for Villarreal, and by the 2022-23 season was one of the most exciting young forwards in La Liga.

Chelsea came calling in the summer of 2023, signing Jackson for £32 million. The move was a gamble on both sides. Chelsea were in the middle of a chaotic rebuild under Todd Boehly, spending heavily on young talent with mixed results. Jackson was raw — quick, direct, capable of spectacular finishes, but also prone to missing chances he should have buried. His first season at Stamford Bridge was a microcosm of that inconsistency: 14 Premier League goals, a hat-trick against Tottenham in November 2023, but also a string of high-profile misses that drew criticism from pundits and fans alike.

Key Moment: Jackson's hat-trick against Tottenham Hotspur on November 6, 2023, was a statement performance. He scored twice in the last 15 minutes to seal a 4-1 win, showing the kind of composure that convinced Chelsea fans they had a special talent on their hands.

Making His Mark at Chelsea

Jackson's second season at Chelsea, 2024-25, was complicated. He scored 10 Premier League goals — a respectable tally but not the leap forward many had hoped for. The Conference League campaign was the real highlight. Jackson scored four goals in the competition, including a decisive goal in the final as Chelsea lifted the trophy, their first European silverware since 2021. He also played in the Club World Cup, though a red card in the semi-final tarnished an otherwise strong tournament. The inconsistency was frustrating, but the talent was undeniable.

By the summer of 2025, Chelsea had a decision to make. They had signed Victor Osimhen for a record fee, and Jackson was no longer guaranteed a starting spot. A loan move to Bayern Munich materialised, and Jackson jumped at the chance."I needed to play regularly, to feel trusted," he said in an interview after the move."Bayern gave me that opportunity. I will always be grateful to Chelsea, but this was the right move for my career." The move would prove transformative.

The Bayern Munich Renaissance

The 2025-26 season at Bayern Munich has been the making of Nicolas Jackson. Playing as the central striker in Vincent Kompany's system, Jackson has flourished in a way he never quite managed at Chelsea. The Bundesliga suits his game: high defensive lines, space to run into, and a team that creates chances by the bucketload. By the winter break, Jackson had already matched his best Premier League goal tally, sitting on 14 Bundesliga goals with half the season remaining.

His form was so good that Bayern reportedly triggered the purchase option in his loan deal before Christmas. Chelsea, who had inserted a buy-back clause in the agreement, watched from afar as their former striker turned into one of Europe's most feared forwards. The transformation was not just about goals — it was about Jackson's overall game. His hold-up play improved, his movement off the ball became sharper, and his finishing, once erratic, had become clinical. The raw talent had matured into genuine world-class ability.

The AFCON Campaign

The Africa Cup of Nations 2025 (held from December 2025 to January 2026) presented Jackson with the perfect platform to showcase his development on the international stage. Senegal entered the tournament as one of the favourites, having won AFCON in 2022 and reached the Round of 16 in the 2022 World Cup. The team still revolved around Sadio Mane — now playing in Saudi Arabia but still the heartbeat of the Lions of Teranga — but there was a sense that this tournament might mark a changing of the guard. Jackson, at 24, was the heir apparent.

Senegal's opening match set the tone. Facing a determined opponent in the group stage, Jackson scored twice in the first half, both finishes showcasing the qualities that had made him a star at Bayern. The first was a typical Jackson goal — a burst of pace to get in behind the defence, a composed finish across the goalkeeper. The second was even better: a quick one-two on the edge of the box followed by a powerful strike into the top corner. Senegal won 3-0, and Jackson walked off with the man of the match award and the match ball. The Golden Boot race had its early leader.

"Scoring for your country is the best feeling in the world. Two goals in our first game — I could not ask for a better start. But we are here to win the tournament, not just individual awards." — Nicolas Jackson, speaking after Senegal's AFCON 2025 opening match

Can He Actually Do It?

The question of Jackson winning both Golden Boots in the same season comes down to timing and mathematics. The Premier League season runs from August to May. The Bundesliga follows the same calendar. AFCON 2025, however, is being played from December 2025 to January 2026 — right in the middle of the European season. Jackson had to leave Bayern for up to a month to represent Senegal, potentially disrupting his momentum and missing Bundesliga matches while his rivals kept scoring.

The Premier League Golden Boot race is a separate complication. Jackson is on loan at Bayern Munich, not in the Premier League. But the qualification for the"Premier League Golden Boot" in Jackson's case would come from the fact that he is a Chelsea player on loan — he remains a Premier League-registered player who happens to be playing abroad for the season. The Premier League Golden Boot is awarded to the player who scores the most goals in the Premier League itself. For Jackson to win it, he would need to either return to Chelsea in January and go on a remarkable scoring run, or the award would need to be reframed as a broader"European Golden Shoe" conversation. The Chelsea Connection

It is impossible to discuss Jackson's rise without acknowledging Chelsea's role in it. The £32 million signing from Villarreal was a statement of intent, but Chelsea have rarely been a forgiving environment for young strikers. The pressure at Stamford Bridge is immense. The fanbase demands immediate results. The manager changes — Jackson played under Mauricio Pochettino, Enzo Maresca, and then saw Chelsea move on again — created instability that made consistent development difficult.

Yet Jackson delivered moments of genuine brilliance in a Chelsea shirt. The hat-trick against Tottenham in November 2023 will be remembered as the day Chelsea fans knew they had something special. The Conference League final goal in 2025 was a moment of pure composure, slotting home under pressure to seal the trophy. And even the red card in the Club World Cup was the kind of mistake that young players make — a learning experience rather than a character flaw. Chelsea fans remain divided on whether letting him go was the right decision, but few doubt that Jackson is now fulfilling the potential they saw in him.

What Would It Mean?

For Jackson, winning both Golden Boots would be a career-defining achievement. It would place him in the conversation with the very best African strikers of all time — Drogba, Eto'o, Salah, Mane, Weah. For Senegal, it would be another chapter in a golden era that began with the 2002 World Cup quarter-final and continued through the AFCON 2022 triumph. Jackson has the potential to be the face of Senegalese football for the next decade. A double Golden Boot season would accelerate that timeline dramatically.

For African football more broadly, Jackson's success would be a powerful statement. The Premier League Golden Boot has been won by African players before — Drogba (twice), Salah (three times), Mane (shared), Aubameyang (shared). But no African player has ever simultaneously dominated both the European scoring charts and the continental tournament in the same season. Jackson has the chance to do something that no one has done before. And if his current form is anything to go by, he might just pull it off.

The Verdict

Can Nicolas Jackson win both the Premier League (Bundesliga) Golden Boot and the AFCON Golden Boot in the same season? The numbers suggest it is not just possible — it is probable. He is scoring at a rate that puts him among Europe's elite, and his start to AFCON 2025 could not have been stronger. The hardest part may be maintaining that form through the disruption of tournament football in the middle of the club season. But Jackson has shown this year that he has the mentality to handle pressure. The raw teenager who arrived at Chelsea in 2023 has become a composed, clinical finisher who belongs on the biggest stages.

Whether he pulls off the double or not, 2025-26 will be remembered as the season Nicolas Jackson announced himself as one of the best strikers in world football.

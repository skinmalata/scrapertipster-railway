The Lowest Attendance at a Major Football Match

Some matches are played in front of 80,000 fans. A few famous ones were played in front of almost nobody. These are the lowest attendances ever recorded in major football.

Football is defined by its crowds. The sport's biggest matches pack 90,000 people into a stadium and broadcast to billions. But not every match is a sell-out, and a handful of fixtures have gone down in history for the opposite reason — the tiny, almost-empty grounds they were played in front of.

The Record: Wimbledon's 3,039

The lowest attendance ever recorded for a Premier League match is 3,039, set when Wimbledon hosted Everton at Selhurst Park on 26 January 1993. The match, which took place on a Tuesday night, drew a crowd smaller than many non-league games — and the figure has never been beaten since, even as the league has grown into the most watched in the world.

Why Was It So Empty?

Wimbledon's crowds were low for reasons that had nothing to do with quality. The club, originally from south London, shared Selhurst Park with Crystal Palace, meaning home fans watched their team play on another club's ground miles away. The 1992-93 season also saw Wimbledon struggle through a bleak midwinter run of fixtures, and a Tuesday night against mid-table Everton was not the fixture to fill the stands.

The result: an attendance so low that it became a trivia staple, and one that has resisted every challenge in the three decades since.

The Rest of the All-Time Low List

Wimbledon dominate the Premier League's all-time lowest-attendance list. The ten lowest crowds in the league's history were all Wimbledon home games in the 1992-93 and 1993-94 seasons, including:

Oldham Athletic's 3,386 and Coventry City's 3,759 are the famous near-misses — clubs whose big crowds at other grounds only made their empty nights more striking.

The 'Zero Attendance' Era

In the modern era, the lowest-attendance record was redefined in a different way. During the COVID-19 pandemic, leagues across the world played matches behind closed doors — with an official attendance of zero. The 2019-20 and 2020-21 seasons of the Premier League, La Liga and most of Europe's leagues were completed entirely in empty stadiums, meaning the 'lowest attendance' in football history, in a technical sense, is now zero.

Those matches are usually treated as a special case — nobody counts a pandemic lockdown as a genuine attendance record. But they prove a point: football can exist without a crowd, just not very well.

Why Low Crowds Matter

An empty stadium tells a story. It can signal a club in decline, a fixture nobody wants to see, or simply a club playing home games in the wrong place. Wimbledon's low crowds were a symptom of their ground-share; the club's subsequent relocation to Milton Keynes and the fans' rebellion that followed became one of the defining stories of English football's modern era.

The bottom line: The record-low Premier League attendance of 3,039, set by Wimbledon in January 1993, has stood for more than 30 years — and with the league's global popularity, it is unlikely ever to be beaten.

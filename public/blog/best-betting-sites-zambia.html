Zambia runs a regulated betting market under the Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB), but licensing is fragmented and offshore sites still compete for players. Here is how to pick a bookmaker you can actually trust with your ZMW in 2026.

Zambian bettors face a market that is regulated but messy. Sports betting is legal through operators licensed under the Betting Control Act, Cap. 166, and the Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB) sits under the Ministry of Finance and National Planning. Yet the sector is split across multiple laws and institutions, casinos are licensed separately by the Ministry of Tourism, and a wave of offshore brands with Curacao licences still serve Zambian players.

Licensing is more than a badge. A local BCLB licence gives you a legal company to complain to, a regulator that can intervene, and a paper trail backed by Zambian law. An offshore Curacao licence regulates the operator somewhere else and gives you little local remedy if a withdrawal is refused.

This guide ranks the bookmakers we could verify for Zambian bettors in 2026, weighting licence evidence, ZMW cashier availability, and whether Airtel Money and MTN MoMo work for both deposits and withdrawals.

Know the Rules: BCLB Licensing and the 2026 Levy

The BCLB issues betting licences for online sports betting and lotteries, while land-based casinos fall under the Casino Licensing Committee in the Ministry of Tourism. Roughly 18 operators currently hold an active licence in Zambia. In the past, enforcement against offshore operators was lax; in 2026 the BCLB is actively working to block unlicensed sites targeting Zambian players, so the risk of using a random brand is rising.

A major tax overhaul took effect on 1 January 2026. The Betting Levy Act No. 27 of 2025 replaced the old presumptive taxes with a 5% levy on every customer deposit and every withdrawal, paid by the betting company. Winnings also carry a 15% withholding tax. That means the operator, not you, absorbs the levy, but payout speed and terms matter more than ever when real money is moving both directions.

How to verify a licence: record the exact company named in the operator's terms, the licence number, the issuing authority and its validity, then confirm Zambia and ZMW are accepted in your logged-in cashier. A reference to a foreign Curacao or Malta licence does not equal Zambian authorisation.

Responsible Gambling in Zambia

The 15% winnings withholding and the new 5% levies are reminders to treat betting as entertainment, not income. Set a monthly deposit budget, never bet money you need for living costs, and use the self-exclusion tools any licensed operator must provide. If a licensed bookmaker misbehaves, the BCLB is the escalation route; for offshore-only brands, you have no local authority to appeal to.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is online betting legal in Zambia?

Yes, for operators licensed by the Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB) under the Betting Control Act, Cap. 166. The minimum age is 18. Many offshore Curacao-licensed sites also accept Zambian players, but they are not locally regulated.

Which betting sites accept Airtel Money in Zambia?

Betwinner, 1xBet and Melbet support Airtel Money for Zambia accounts, alongside MTN MoMo and Zamtel Kwacha. Always confirm the method appears in both the Deposit and Withdraw menus of your own logged-in cashier, because availability varies per account.

Are my betting winnings taxed in Zambia?

Winnings are subject to a 15% withholding tax. In addition, since 1 January 2026 the Betting Levy Act 27 of 2025 charges betting companies a 5% levy on customer deposits and 5% on withdrawals — an operator-side cost, not a charge on your balance.

Why is betting regulation in Zambia confusing?

Because it is fragmented. The BCLB licenses online betting and lotteries, while casinos are licensed separately by the Casino Licensing Committee under the Ministry of Tourism, and the Zambia Revenue Authority administers gambling taxes. There is no single regulator for everything.

Can I use offshore betting sites in Zambia?

They are technically accessible and several (1xBet, Melbet, Linebet) have ZMW cashiers, but offshore licences give you no local complaint route. The BCLB has been increasing efforts to block unlicensed domains targeting Zambian players since 2026.

Free Bet Strategy: How to Extract Maximum Value

A £20 free bet is not worth £20. Learn how the maths works, and how to convert promotional offers into the highest guaranteed value possible.

Every bookmaker offers free bets — for signing up, for depositing, for losing a tight game by one goal. They look like free money. They are not. A free bet converts to cash only when it wins, and the way bookmakers structure them guarantees that the average conversion is a fraction of the headline number.

Why a Free Bet Is Not Worth Its Face Value

When you place a free bet, you receive the winnings only — the stake is not returned. Back a 5.00 selection with a £20 free bet and a win returns £80 (4 x £20). Compare that with a real £20 stake, which returns £100. The missing £20 is the stake you never get back, and that gap is the tax on every free bet.

The conversion rate is roughly the inverse of the odds. The higher the price you use the free bet on, the higher the percentage of the face value you can capture — up to a point. At even money (2.00), you recover half of the value. At 5.00, you recover 80%. At 10.00, 90% — but the variance becomes wild.

Conversion rule: A free bet on a 2.00 selection converts, on average, to ~50% of its face value. On a 3.00 selection, ~67%. The best risk-adjusted conversions sit around 70-80%.

The Guaranteed Conversion (Matched Approach)

The professional way to convert a free bet is to make it risk-free by covering the opposite outcome elsewhere. Use the free bet on one side of a market and a real stake on the other side of an exchange or a second bookmaker. Whatever happens, one side pays, and the net result is a guaranteed capture of most of the free bet's value.

  1. Place the free bet on a selection at a price of roughly 3.00 or higher.
  2. Place a real stake on the same selection losing at the exchange (lay the same team).
  3. Calculate the lay stake so that both outcomes yield the same profit.
  4. Collect the fixed amount regardless of the result.

This is the core of matched betting, and it converts welcome offers into near-guaranteed profit in a single pass.

Bet-and-Get vs Bet-and-Build Offers

Not all offers behave the same. Bet-and-get rewards deposit and staking activity, usually after qualifying wagering. Bet-and-build (also called stake-back) returns your stake as a free bet if your bet loses — a much richer structure because the downside is removed entirely. When choosing offers, prefer stake-back mechanics and refund offers over credit-based ones.

The Fine Print That Decides Value

ConditionEffect on value
Min odds to qualifyPushes you to higher-risk bets
Wagering requirementMust re-stake winnings before withdrawal

Max stakeCaps how much you can convert
Excluded marketsBlocks the best conversion prices

Read every term before placing. A "no deposit free bet" with a 40x wagering requirement is worth almost nothing, no matter how large the headline number.

Maximising Without Matched Betting

If you do not want to run a matching process, the smart casual play is: use free bets on higher-priced selections (3.00 to 6.00) where you have genuine value, accept the variance, and treat the offer as a one-off — never let a free bet persuade you to bet on matches you have not researched.

Final Thoughts

Free bets are marketing spend, and the bookmaker's whole design is to make you treat them like winnings. The disciplined bettor treats them like what they are: a coupon worth 50-80% of face value, convertible at best through a matched approach or a well-chosen value price. Take every offer that genuinely converts, skip every one that requires you to gamble for the privilege, and let the fine print decide — not the banner.

Converting at Different Prices

The conversion maths is worth making concrete. A £20 free bet at 2.00 returns £20 of winnings when it lands — half the face value. At 3.00 it returns £40, or 67% of face value. At 5.00 it returns £80, or 80%. The pattern tempts bettors toward longer prices, but variance punishes it: a 5.00 free bet lands only one time in five, and the guaranteed-conversion approaches quoted above still work best in the 2.50-4.00 band where the lay side is priced sensibly.

The matching calculation for a 3.00 free bet with a £20 face value is straightforward. Lay the same selection at the exchange at 3.10. A backer matches the lay, so the exchange requires you to cover their stake. If the selection wins, the free bet pays £40 and the lay pays out your liability; if it loses, the lay keeps the backer's stake minus commission. Sized correctly, both branches return roughly £26-27 — a 70% conversion with no outcome risk. The exact figure depends on the commission and the lay price, which is why a conversion calculator is a free-bet bettor's most used tool. Anything converting above 70% is a good offer; anything below 55% is probably a trap dressed in fine print.

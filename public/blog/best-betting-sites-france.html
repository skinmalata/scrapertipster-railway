Best Betting Sites in France 2026: ANJ-Licensed Bookmakers Ranked

France runs one of the most tightly regulated online betting markets in Europe. Sports betting, horse racing and poker are legal under five-year ANJ licences; online casino is not. Here are the best licensed bookmakers for 2026, plus what the 2025 tax hike means for your winnings.

French betting is a paradox: one of the most valuable markets in Europe, and one of the most constrained. Every legal online bookmaker holds a five-year licence from the Autorité Nationale des Jeux (ANJ), all of them are subject to a tax burden that reached about 59.3% of gross gaming revenue in mid-2025, and online casino remains completely illegal. What players get in return is serious protection: a listed, accountable operator, no tax on winnings, and a regulator that blocks illegal sites chasing French customers. Here is the best of the legal market in 2026.

Know the Rules: ANJ, the Five Verticals and the Casino Ban

The legal framework dates to the 2010 law that liberalised online gambling, and the ANJ has overseen it since June 2020, when it replaced ARJEL with a wider mandate covering market supervision, advertising and player protection. Online sports betting, horse-race betting and poker are legal and licensed; online casino is not - a proposed legalisation was rolled into the 2025 budget process and then withdrawn in October 2024, leaving France as one of the last two EU states (with Cyprus) to ban it.

Licences run for five years and are renewable. In retail, the FDJ holds the lottery monopoly and the PMU the horse-racing monopoly; online, operator licences apply to the .fr-visible brands themselves, and hundreds of foreign-licensed sites serving France without a licence - including biggest offshore casino names - have been DNS-blocked by the ANJ, most recently in a wave in August 2025.

Tax: The 2025 Hike and Why It Matters

From 1 July 2025 the effective tax on online sports betting rose to roughly 59.3% of gross gaming revenue, built from a 33.7% levy, a social contribution (CSG) that moved from 10.6% to 15%, and a 6.6% sport-integrity levy; poker moved to 10% of GGR and horse racing sits around 52.9%. A new 15% tax applies to gambling advertising and sponsorship spend. For players the consequence is a slightly thinner margin on the board - licensed operators pass on part of the cost - but your winnings are not taxed, and your deposits are protected by the ANJ process. Payments for French Bettors

Licensed French operators offer card payments (CB, Visa, Mastercard), SEPA bank transfers from named accounts and e-wallet options such as Skrill and Neteller, with PayPal available at several sites. Crypto is prohibited across the licensed market, and deposits must come from verified accounts in your own name. Because the ANJ keeps foreign sites blocked and payment processors refuse unlicensed merchants, betting through a licensed operator is both the safe and the practical route in France.

Bonuses and What the 2025 Rules Changed

French welcome offers are competitive - typically a matched-bet bonus up to a few hundred euros with wagering in the 20 times range - but the 2025 advertising tax and the general squeeze on margins mean the terms have tightened. Always read the qualifying odds and time limits, and remember that a 59.3% tax sits behind the board: the operator, not you, pays it, but it shapes the prices on offer. A bonus that pushes you to bet more than your usual bankroll is marketing, not value.

Responsible Gambling in France

The ANJ requires every licensed operator to carry risk-detection tools, and France operates the interdit de jeux national self-exclusion register alongside the Evaluju platform of self-tests. From early 2027 new tools are expected to let the ANJ set mandatory loss, deposit and time limits for young adults aged 18-25. The practical advice is the same as everywhere: stake within a strict monthly budget, hold lines for value rather than action, and if betting stops being entertainment, call the national gambling helpline - every licensed French site must display it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is online sports betting legal in France?

Yes, for licensed operators. Sports betting, horse racing and poker are legal online under five-year ANJ licences. About 16-17 operators hold licences, and all must be registered with the ANJ and serve French players from compliant rails.

Is online casino legal in France?

No. Online casino remains prohibited in France - a 2024 attempt to legalise it was withdrawn in October 2024. France is one of only two EU states (with Cyprus) that still ban online casino, and the ANJ blocks foreign casino sites.

Which betting sites are licensed in France?

Licensed sportsbooks include Winamax, Betclic, Unibet, ParionsSport en Ligne (FDJ), bet365, bwin, Vbet and PMU among others. The ANJ publishes the official registered-operator list at anj.fr.

Do I pay tax on betting winnings in France?

Recreational players do not pay tax on winnings from licensed operators. Instead the operator pays roughly 59.3% of gross gaming revenue in levies on online sports betting since 1 July 2025, plus a 15% tax on advertising and sponsorship.

What is the ANJ and what does it do for players?

The ANJ is the independent gambling regulator created in June 2020. It licenses operators, supervises the market, enforces advertising and player-protection rules, maintains the interdit de jeux self-exclusion register and blocks unlicensed sites in France.

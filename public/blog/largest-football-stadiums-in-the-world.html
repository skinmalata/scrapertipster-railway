The Largest Football Stadiums in the World

Football's biggest grounds hold more people than many towns. From a 114,000-seat colossus in North Korea to the cathedrals of Europe, here are the largest football stadiums in the world.

Stadiums are football's cathedrals, and a few of them are genuinely colossal. The biggest grounds in the world are measured in the tens of thousands of seats — and one in North Korea holds more spectators than any stadium on Earth has ever held for football.

The World's Largest: Rungrado 1st of May Stadium

The largest football stadium in the world is the Rungrado 1st of May Stadium in Pyongyang, North Korea, with an official capacity of 114,000. Opened in 1989, its huge scalloped roof is shaped like magnolia petals, and it is best known for hosting the Arirang mass games rather than regular club football. It is the only stadium on Earth where the crowd alone could fill a small city.

Camp Nou: The Biggest Club Stadium

Europe's largest stadium is Barcelona's Camp Nou, which holds 99,354 people and is the biggest club-owned football ground in the world. It has been the scene of more than 60 years of the club's most famous nights. The stadium is currently undergoing a major renovation as Barcelona prepare for the 2030 World Cup, with the rebuilt capacity expected to reach around 105,000 — which would put it within touching distance of Rungrado.

Wembley: The Home of English Football

London's Wembley Stadium is the largest in England, seating 90,000 beneath its iconic arch. It is the spiritual home of the England national team and has hosted the final of the 1966 World Cup, the 2012 Olympic football and the European Championship, including the dramatic Euro 2020 final. Its 90,000 seats make it one of the ten biggest football stadiums anywhere.

The 2026 World Cup Factor

The 2026 World Cup, co-hosted by the United States, Mexico and Canada, brought a new set of giant venues into the football spotlight. The final was played at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey — an 82,500-seat stadium that became the biggest World Cup final venue in history. Estadio Azteca in Mexico City achieved its own piece of history by becoming the first stadium to host World Cup matches in three different editions (1970, 1986 and 2026).

Why Bigger Isn't Always Better

Capacity is a matter of pride, but the biggest stadiums are rarely the most atmospheric. Clubs like Barcelona and Juventus have discussed or built smaller, more intimate grounds in recent years because a full 40,000-seat bowl generates more noise than a half-empty 90,000-seat one. The loudest football crowds in history, after all, have come from stadiums far smaller than Rungrado.

Quick answer: The largest football stadium in the world is Rungrado 1st of May Stadium (114,000). The largest club stadium is Camp Nou (99,354). The biggest World Cup final venue ever was MetLife Stadium (82,500) in 2026.

The Substitute Who Scored Every Time He Came Off the Bench

12 min read

Tip: Betting on substitute goalscorers? Anytime goalscorer markets offer great value on super-subs who change games from the bench.

There are footballers who build their legend in the starting eleven, and then there is Nils Petersen — a man who built his on the substitutes' bench. For over a decade, he was the most dangerous weapon in the Bundesliga not because of what he did in the first minute, but because of what he did in the 70th, the 80th, and the 90th. He came off the bench to score 34 Bundesliga goals. No one in league history has scored more.

And here is the part that sounds made up: he scored within 60 seconds of coming on in his final ever professional match — a Champions League qualifying clincher for Freiburg in May 2025. The perfect ending to a career defined by impossible timing.

"I don't know what it is. I come on, the ball falls to me, and I score. It's not a tactic. It's a feeling." — Nils Petersen

The Making of a Super-Sub

Petersen did not start out as a bench specialist. He came through at Energie Cottbus before earning a move to Bayern Munich in 2011. He moved to Werder Bremen in 2013 and then to SC Freiburg in 2015, where he found his home. He scored 12 goals in his first full season and helped Freiburg earn promotion. But it was after returning to the top flight that his super-sub identity truly emerged. He was not always first choice. But when Freiburg needed a goal, Christian Streich sent for Petersen. And he delivered. Again. And again. And again.

The Psychology of the Bench

What made Petersen so effective? Part of it was technical — his extraordinary sense of timing in the box, fresh legs against tired defenders. But the real answer is psychological. He embraced the role. He studied the game from the bench, watched the centre-backs, noted their patterns."I prefer coming off the bench in the second half," he told Der Spiegel in 2022. "As a starter, you deal with intensity from the first minute. As a substitute, you know exactly what you are walking into."

The numbers back him up. At his peak, Petersen scored at a rate of one goal every 98 minutes as a substitute. Robert Lewandowski, the best pure striker of his generation, scored roughly one every 110 minutes during his prime. Petersen was scoring more frequently off the bench than the best striker in the world scored as a starter.

Context: Jermain Defoe holds the Premier League record with 24 goals as a substitute. Ole Gunnar Solskjaer had 17 Premier League sub goals. Petersen's 34 Bundesliga goals off the bench outpaces both — a testament to his extraordinary consistency in a role most strikers despise.

The second leg was goalless heading into the final 20 minutes. A Champions League group stage place — the holy grail for a club of Freiburg's size — hung in the balance. Streich turned to his bench. He called for Petersen.

The striker entered in the 72nd minute. Less than sixty seconds later, the ball fell to him in the box. He finished — the same way he had finished 34 other times off the bench. The stadium erupted. Freiburg held on. The club qualified for the Champions League group stage for the first time in its history. And Nils Petersen walked off the pitch as a professional footballer for the final time, having scored the most perfectly timed goal of them all.

"You could not have written that script. It was pure cinema. That moment was everything Nils has been for us." — Christian Streich, Freiburg head coach

Legacy

Petersen finished his Bundesliga career with 89 goals, 34 from the bench. He never scored more than 10 in a season as a starter. He was not on billboards. He did not command huge wages. But ask any Bundesliga defender who the most dangerous forward to face in the final 20 minutes was, and they will tell you: Nils Petersen. He was not the best striker in Germany. He was the best at delivering in the moments that mattered most.

In English football, the super-sub is a cherished archetype — Jermain Defoe (24 PL sub goals), Ole Gunnar Solskjaer (17), David Fairclough — players who carved careers out of late-game interventions. Petersen belongs in that conversation, and arguably on top of it. No one in Bundesliga history has done it better. And no one has ended their career with a more fitting final chapter.

Betting Insight: The Super-Sub Market

Substitute goalscorers offer some of the best value in live betting. As games open up and defenders tire, fresh legs from the bench exploit gaps that did not exist in the first hour. Petersen's career proves that certain players have a repeatable knack for this — and bookmakers often undervalue it. When betting on anytime goalscorer markets, always check the bench. A proven super-sub coming on against a tired defence at 1-0 or 0-0 is often the best bet on the board.

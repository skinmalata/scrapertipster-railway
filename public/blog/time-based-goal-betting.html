Time-Based Betting: Wagering on Goals in Specific Minutes

Updated: 2026 | 9 min read

Tip: Looking for today's football predictions? Click here.

Among the most nuanced betting markets in football are time-based bets — wagers tied to when something happens rather than if it happens. These markets are offered by Bet365, Betway, and 1xBet, and they reward bettors who understand the temporal patterns of football matches.

What Are Time-Based Betting Markets?

Time-based betting covers any market where timing is the primary condition. Examples include:

  • First goal scored before/after a specific minute
  • Goal in a specific 15-minute interval (e.g., 15:00–30:00)
  • Last goal before half-time
  • Goal in the last 10 minutes of a match
  • Goal in the 76–90 minute window (often called the"insurance goal" market)
Example — "Goal in 15:01–30:00":

You bet that a goal will be scored between 15 minutes and 30 minutes (the exact second doesn't matter).
The odds might be 2.50.

If the first goal comes at 22' — you win. If it's 8' or 33' — you lose.
Goals after 30' don't affect this bet; only the FIRST goal matters.

How Bookmakers Set Time-Based Lines

Bookmakers use aggregate data from thousands of matches to set these lines. They know that:

  • Goals are most likely in the first 15 minutes (teams are fresh, pressing high)
  • Goals spike again in the last 15 minutes (fatigue, desperation, open games)
  • The 15–30 and 30–45 minute windows are typically lower-scoring

Research published in sports science journals confirms that goal distribution follows a U-shaped curve — more goals early and late, fewer in the middle.

Key Factors That Influence Goal Timing

1. Team Pace of Play

High-pressing teams score earlier on average. Teams that take their time building up play (like many Italian teams) see more goals in the second half. Check a team's average first goal time on SofaScore.

2. Defensive Quality vs Attacking Intent

A match between two defensive teams is likely to stay goalless early. The longer a 0-0 draw continues, the more both teams may open up — shifting goal probability toward the final 30 minutes.

3. Match Context

Must-win matches see earlier goals — teams come out flying. Relegation six-pointers and cup finals historically see earlier goals than mid-table dead rubbers.

4. Weather

Heavy rain and wind slow the game, reduce early goal attempts, and push goals later. Check the forecast on BBC Weather before betting on early time markets.

The"No Goal by X Minute" Strategy

If no goal is scored in the first 20 minutes, the"Goal before 30:00" market on Bet365 often becomes great value. At 20 minutes, if both teams are creating chances but the scoresheet remains blank, the market hasn't caught up to the play. Back"Goal before 30:00" at inflated odds while the game is still goalless.

Pro Tip: Check First-Goal Average Times

FBref provides detailed timing stats. Look at each team's average first goal minute across the season. A team averaging 28 minutes for their first goal has a different timing profile than one averaging 44 minutes. Use this to find edges in first-goal minute markets.

Popular Time Betting Strategies

1. Late Goal Backing (76+ Minutes)

Matches with low xG in the first half but high second-half energy — back the"Goal in 76–90" market. The value comes from the market underestimating the push for a late equaliser or winner.

2. Interval Split Betting

Instead of one time bet, split your stake across two intervals. E.g., if you think a goal is likely between 15–30, cover 0–15 as well with a smaller stake. This increases your coverage while keeping some upside.

3. In-Play Time Correction

In-play time markets adjust live. If it goes 60' and the line is set at "Goal by 75", the odds are often generous because the market overshoots. A goal is most likely to come in the final stretch of a match — this is where in-play time betting has consistent value.

Common Mistakes

Betting on first-half intervals without checking team form: A slow-starting team facing an aggressive press means the early interval may not have the expected goal frequency.

Ignoring added time: The 90th minute is actually closer to the 93rd in modern football. Time markets can end at 90:00 or 90:05 depending on the bookmaker — check the rules.

The Bottom Line

Time-based betting rewards bettors who understand goal distribution patterns, team tempo tendencies, and match context. Use first-goal average data from FBref and SofaScore, and look for in-play opportunities where the market hasn't caught up to the actual play on the pitch.

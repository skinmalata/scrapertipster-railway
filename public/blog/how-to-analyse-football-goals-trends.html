How to Analyse Football Goals Trends

Published July 26, 2026 · 11 min read

Goals are the currency of football, and understanding how they are scored, when they are scored, and by whom is the foundation of smart betting on goals markets. Analysing goals trends goes beyond simply checking how many goals a team has scored this season. It involves looking at patterns across time, across different match situations, and across different opponents to build a complete picture of a team's scoring profile.

This guide gives you a structured approach to analysing goals trends that you can apply to any league or match.

Why Goals Trends Matter for Betting

The most popular football betting markets are goals-based. Over/under goals, both teams to score, correct score, and half-time goals all depend on understanding how goals are likely to be distributed in a match. If you can accurately predict whether a match will produce goals, you have an edge in the majority of available markets.

Goals trends reveal patterns that raw averages miss. A team might average 2.5 goals per match overall, but that average could be driven by a few high-scoring games against weak opponents, with many low-scoring matches against stronger teams. Understanding the distribution, not just the average, gives you a more accurate picture.

The Key Metrics

Several metrics form the foundation of goals trend analysis. Each one tells you something different about a team's scoring profile.

Essential Goals Metrics

  • Goals per match (GF and GA): The most basic metric. How many goals does a team score and concede on average?
  • Goals per match home vs away: Many teams score significantly more at home than away. This split is critical for accurate prediction.
  • Goals in first half vs second half: Some teams are fast starters, others grow into matches. This affects half-time goals markets.
  • Over/under percentages: What percentage of a team's matches go over or under 2.5 goals? This is more useful than the raw average for over/under betting.
  • BTTS percentage: What percentage of a team's matches see both teams score? This directly informs BTTS betting.
  • Expected goals (xG): How many goals a team should have scored based on the quality of their chances. This is more predictive than actual goals because it strips out finishing variance.

Building a Goals Profile

The most effective way to analyse goals trends is to build a comprehensive goals profile for each team you are betting on. This involves gathering data across multiple dimensions and looking for patterns.

  1. Start with the season average. How many goals per match does the team score and concede overall? This gives you the baseline.
  2. Split by home and away. Check the team's goals per match at home and away separately. A team averaging 2.8 goals at home but 1.2 away is a very different proposition depending on the venue.
  3. Look at the over/under distribution. Check the percentage of matches going over 2.5 goals. A team with 60% over 2.5 rate is a strong candidate for goals bets, while one with 35% is not.
  4. Analyze the BTTS rate. A team with a 70% BTTS rate is involved in open, attacking matches regardless of the result. This is directly useful for BTTS betting.
  5. Check the last 10 matches. Has the team's goals profile changed recently? A team that was averaging 3.0 goals but has scored only 1.2 in their last five matches might have a tactical issue or injury problem.
  6. Consider the opponent. Some teams' goals profiles change dramatically against different types of opponents. Check how the team performs against strong, medium, and weak defences.

Identifying Goal Scoring Patterns

Beyond the raw numbers, goals trends reveal patterns in how and when teams score. These patterns can give you an edge in specific markets.

  • Early scorers: Some teams score disproportionately in the first 15 minutes. This makes them good candidates for "team to score first" bets and first-half goals markets.
  • Late bloomers: Teams that score frequently after 75 minutes tend to be involved in more goals late in matches. This creates value in the over 0.5 goals after 75 minutes market.
  • Set-piece specialists: Teams that score a high percentage of goals from set pieces tend to maintain their scoring rate even against strong defences, because set pieces are less affected by defensive organisation.
  • Counter-attacking teams: Teams that score primarily on the counter-attack tend to score fewer goals against weaker teams (who sit deep) and more against stronger teams (who push forward). This reversal of the expected pattern creates betting opportunities.
  • Consistent scorers vs volatile scorers: Some teams score in almost every match but rarely score more than two. Others are boom-or-bust, scoring three or four in some matches and none in others. Consistent scorers are better for over 0.5 or over 1.5 bets, while volatile scorers are better for over 2.5 bets.

Pro Tip: Track Goals Conceded by Time Period

Goals conceded trends are just as important as goals scored trends. A team that concedes frequently in the first 15 minutes is vulnerable to early goals, making "opponent to score first" a valuable bet. A team that concedes frequently after 75 minutes shows late-game concentration issues, creating value for late goals markets.

Using xG to Refine Your Analysis

Expected goals (xG) is a more sophisticated metric that measures the quality of chances created and conceded, rather than just the actual goals. xG reveals whether a team's scoring rate is sustainable or whether it is being driven by luck.

xG vs Actual Goals

  • Team A: 50 goals scored from 42 xG. Overperforming by 8 goals. Likely to regress towards their xG in future matches.
  • Team B: 38 goals scored from 45 xG. Underperforming by 7 goals. Likely to improve as their finishing regresses to the mean.

Betting against Team A (expecting fewer goals) and backing Team B (expecting more goals) is a data-driven approach that often offers value before the market adjusts.

Practical Application

The real value of goals trend analysis comes from applying it systematically to your betting. Before placing any goals-based bet, work through the goals profile of both teams. Check the home/away splits, the over/under percentages, the BTTS rates, and the xG data. Only place the bet when the data supports your thesis.

This discipline will not make every bet a winner, but it will ensure that your bets are grounded in evidence rather than gut feeling. Over time, this evidence-based approach is the difference between profitable and unprofitable betting.

Throw-Ins Betting: Niche Statistical Market

Last updated: June 2026 | 12 min read

Tip: Looking for today's football predictions? Click here.

Throw-in betting is one of the most niche markets in football wagering. While corners, cards, and goal markets dominate the betting landscape, throw-in markets remain an overlooked opportunity that offers genuine value for bettors willing to put in the research. Because so few bettors engage with throw-in markets, bookmaker lines are often set using simplistic averages rather than deep tactical analysis.

This guide provides a comprehensive framework for throw-in betting — covering the markets available, the tactical factors that influence throw-in counts, team-level analysis, league differences, and where to place your bets for the best odds.

What Are Throw-In Betting Markets?

Throw-in markets operate similarly to corner and offside markets. Bookmakers set a line for the total number of throw-ins in a match (typically ranging from 40–55), and you bet over or under that line. The markets available include:

  • Total match throw-ins over/under — The most common market, typically with lines of 44.5, 49.5, or 54.5 depending on the teams
  • Team-specific throw-ins over/under — Each team's individual throw-in count
  • Asian total throw-ins — Quarter and half-ball lines for push possibilities, primarily at Asian bookmakers
  • First-half throw-ins over/under — Available at select books like 1xBet

What Drives Throw-In Counts?

Throw-ins are not random events. They follow predictable patterns based on playing style, formation, match context, and opponent behaviour. Understanding these drivers is essential for developing a profitable throw-in betting strategy.

1. Possession Style

Possession-based teams that dominate the ball and build play methodically tend to generate more throw-ins in the opponent's half. Teams like Manchester City, Barcelona, and Arsenal regularly force the opposition to clear the ball out of play, creating offensive throw-ins. However, they also concede fewer throw-ins defensively because they keep the ball away from their own third.

2. Wing Play and Crossing

Teams that attack predominantly through wide areas produce more throw-ins. Every cross that is blocked, every dribble that runs out of play near the byline, and every pass that goes wide generates a throw-in. Check WhoScored for each team's cross-per-game and wide-pass volume.

3. Pressing Intensity

High-pressing teams force opponents into hurried clearances that often go out of play. Liverpool under Klopp, for example, consistently generated high throw-in counts because their press forced errors and rushed passes into touch. The more a team presses, the more throw-ins they tend to create.

4. Pitch Dimensions

Narrower pitches increase the likelihood of the ball going out of play because there is less space to manoeuvre. Some Premier League stadiums have significantly narrower pitches (e.g., Old Trafford, Selhurst Park), which historically produce higher throw-in counts. Check pitch dimensions before betting — this information is available on Transfermarkt under stadium details.

The Possession vs Pressing Strategy

When a possession-dominant team (like Arsenal) faces a high-pressing team (like Liverpool), the total throw-in count tends to exceed the bookmaker line. Both teams force play into wide areas — Arsenal through controlled buildup, Liverpool through counter-pressing. Historically, these matchups average 50–58 throw-ins, while the typical line sits around 49.5. The over is consistently valuable.

Match Context Impact on Throw-Ins

The game state dramatically affects throw-in counts. Understanding these situational factors gives you an edge over bookmaker algorithms.

Scoreline Effect

When the underdog leads, they tend to clear the ball long and wide, increasing throw-ins in their defensive third. Conversely, when the favourite leads, they may slow the game, reducing overall throw-in count. In-play betting on throw-ins is especially valuable after a goal — the market adjusts slowly to the new game state.

Time of Match

Second halves produce more throw-ins than first halves as players tire and technical quality declines. Fatigue leads to more misdirected passes and rushed clearances. SportsbookReview data shows the final 30 minutes of matches produce approximately 35–40% of all throw-ins.

Weather Conditions

Rain and wind significantly increase throw-in counts. A wet pitch causes the ball to slide faster and more frequently out of play. Wind makes long passes unpredictable, driving the ball wide. Check weather forecasts before placing throw-in bets — a rainy match can add 5–8 extra throw-ins to the total.

In-Play Throw-In Betting

Live throw-in betting is where the sharpest edges exist. Here is how to approach it:

Early Tempo Assessment

Watch the first 10–15 minutes. If the match is end-to-end with frequent stoppages, the throw-in pace will exceed the line. If possession is slow and controlled, the under may be the better play. Bookmakers adjust throw-in lines slowly in-play, giving observant bettors a window of opportunity.

Long Throw-In Specialists

Teams with a long throw-in specialist (like Rory Delap's Stoke historically, or teams with players who can launch throw-ins directly into the box) significantly increase their attacking throw-in frequency because they treat throw-ins as set-piece opportunities. Check Transfermarkt for known long-throw players.

Substitution Effects

When a team substitutes a technical midfielder for a defensive midfielder to protect a lead, expect more defensive clearances and more throw-ins conceded. Conversely, bringing on a playmaker may reduce throw-ins as the team retains possession better.

Pro Tip: The First-Half Trap

Many bettors assume throw-ins are evenly distributed between halves, but data from Understat shows that actually 52–55% of throw-ins occur in the second half. If the first half produces 20 or fewer throw-ins, the second half over is very strong value as the game opens up and fatigue sets in.

Building a Statistical Approach

A systematic approach to throw-in betting requires tracking the right data:

  1. Team averages — Track both throw-ins for and throw-ins against over the last 10 matches separately for home and away
  2. Opponent adjustment — Factor in the opponent's tendency to concede throw-ins (some teams force play wide; others allow central penetration)
  3. Pitch width factor — Narrower pitches = more throw-ins. Apply a +/- 5% adjustment based on pitch width
  4. Weather adjustment — Rain/wind = +5% to projected total. Still conditions = -3%
  5. Match importance — Cup finals and high-stakes matches tend to produce fewer throw-ins as teams play cautiously

Worked Example

Match: Brighton vs Tottenham (Premier League)

Line: Over/Under 49.5 throw-ins @ 1.91

Team Data (last 10 matches):
Brighton throw-ins for: 31/game • Against: 24/game • Total: 55
Tottenham throw-ins for: 28/game • Against: 26/game • Total: 54

Combined average: (55 + 54) / 2 = 54.5 projected throw-ins

Pitch adjustment: Amex Stadium is standard width → no change

Weather: Light rain forecast → +2 throw-ins = 56.5 projected

Verdict: Over 49.5 throw-ins has strong value. The projection of 56.5 comfortably exceeds the line of 49.5.

Combining Throw-Ins With Other Markets

Throw-in data can enhance other betting strategies. Consider these combinations:

Throw-Ins + Corners

Matches with high throw-in counts also tend to produce more corners because both metrics are driven by wide play. A same-game multi on over 49.5 throw-ins and over 10.5 corners can provide boosted odds with high correlation.

Throw-Ins + Player Shots

Teams that attack down the wings generate both throw-ins and shots from wide players. If you back over 49.5 throw-ins, consider adding an over on shots by the opposition's wide forwards.

Throw-Ins + Fouls

Throw-ins often result from defensive actions near the touchline, which also produce fouls. High throw-in matches frequently correlate with elevated foul counts.

Common Throw-In Betting Mistakes

Treating all leagues the same: A 49.5 line in La Liga is very different from a 49.5 line in the Championship. Always calibrate to league averages.

Ignoring possession stats: A team with 35% possession cannot generate 30+ throw-ins. Check expected possession before betting team-specific throw-in markets.

Overbetting on cup matches: Throw-in data for cup matches is scarce and unreliable. Stick to league matches with larger sample sizes.

Forgetting about long throws: Teams with long-throw specialists effectively treat throw-ins as set pieces, dramatically increasing their offensive throw-in frequency.

Important: Throw-in markets are less liquid than corners or cards. Use smaller stake sizes relative to your normal unit, as the market can move significantly with minimal volume. Avoid throw-in betting on obscure leagues where data is unreliable.

Throw-Ins Betting FAQ

What is a typical throw-in line for a Premier League match?

Typically 47.5 to 51.5, with 49.5 being the most common. The actual match average is 48–55.

Which bookmaker has the best throw-in markets?

Pinnacle offers the best Asian throw-in markets with quarter-ball lines and the lowest margins. Bet365 offers the widest selection of team-specific throw-in markets.

Do throw-in lines vary by league?

Yes, significantly. The Championship averages 50–58 throw-ins per match, while La Liga averages 42–48. Always research league baselines before betting.

Can I bet on throw-ins in African leagues?

Rarely. Throw-in markets are typically only offered for Europe's top five leagues, the Championship, and major UEFA competitions.

Is there a correlation between possession and throw-ins?

Yes. Teams with higher possession (60%+) generate more throw-ins in the opponent's half but concede fewer in their own half. The net effect is usually a slight increase in total match throw-ins.

How many throw-ins occur in an average football match?

The global average across top European leagues is approximately 46–52 throw-ins per match, varying by league and competition.
